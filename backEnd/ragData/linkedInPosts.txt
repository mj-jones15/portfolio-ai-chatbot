On presenting my policy research at the University of Kentucky, I shared:
"I am so pleased to have presented at the 20th annual University of Kentucky Showcase of Undergraduate Scholars. Thanks are in order to my advisor, Dr. Brent Harrison, for giving me the freedom to pursue a research question I genuinely care about: how AI policy is grounded in research that precedes it. Key findings: 1. China's AI policy ranked highest in thematic alignment with RAI keywords but had zero supporting research abstracts above the threshold. In my dataset, this was the largest gap between policy ambition and research grounding. 2. A three-way misalignment where the largest share of RAI publications are in fairness, the highest cited papers are in explainability, and legislators most often use accountability language. I am excited to continue this work and see where it leads!"

Reflecting on the conclusion of my WPI REU program in Summer 2026, I shared:
"WPI REU Summer 2026! On top of such an exciting summer, my partner and I won best showcase presentation, and I think this is an exemplary culmination of the creative solutions, hard work, and attentive research in which we partook for two months. Through this process I’ll have completed a full project pipeline end-to-end: problem identification, hypothesis, solution design, execution, and dissemination. That’s exactly the kind of investigative rigor I want to bring into a Ph.D., and why I feel this opportunity uniquely positioned me as a qualified candidate for graduate school. To briefly review some of our work’s contributions, we implemented a novel feature selection method, Association Rule Interaction Scoring and Ensemble Selection (ARISES), which uses pharmacovigilance methods to identify and amplify correlation signals in the dataset. Our second contribution, which I designed and implemented, is Multi-Label eXplainer (MLX)—a novel explainability method that uses structured gradient descent on the classifier’s own probability surface to generate counterfactual explanations for combinations of labels, not just one at a time. Where methods like LIME or SHAP explain each adverse event in isolation, MLX explains how labels interact with each other in the model’s prediction. A huge thank you to the whole WPI team, including Professor Ben, our REU PI Elke Rundensteiner, and our program lead Jessica Lusignan. This was a wonderful summer where I learned a lot and grew equally as a human being through travel and new experiences around the Northeast!"
